PDVOICE BUDDY — SPANISH PROOFREADING SHEET
Latin American Spanish (neutral), usted throughout

For a fluent proofreader. Mark up anything that reads badly and send it back —
you do not need to know anything about the app or about code.

HOW TO MARK IT UP
    Write your correction next to any line that's wrong, or just cross it out
    and write what you'd say instead. Free-text is fine. Don't worry about
    matching length exactly, but shorter is better for the items marked
    [TIGHT] — those are drawn in small spaces on screen.


DECISIONS ALREADY MADE — tell us if you disagree
=============================================================================

USTED, NOT TÚ
    The people using this are mostly older adults, and the app is a health
    tool. "Toca para cambiar" felt too familiar, so everything uses usted:
    "Toque para cambiar." Say so if this reads as stiff or cold to you — it's
    a judgement call and we'd rather be corrected now.

"AUTO", NOT "CARRO" OR "COCHE"
    Neutral across most of Latin America. Change it if your region would
    stumble on it.

"APLICACIÓN", NOT "APP"
    Used in the one long sentence where it appears, on the grounds that older
    users may not know "app". Shorten it if you think that's over-cautious.


PART 1 — THE WORDS UNDER THE DOT  [TIGHT]
=============================================================================
These are the most important six lines in the whole app. They're read
mid-conversation, out of the corner of the eye, at a glance. They must be
short, and instantly clear.

    English                     Spanish used            Your correction
    -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Paused                      En pausa                ____________________
    Starting…                   Iniciando…              ____________________
    Listening to the room       Escuchando la sala      ____________________
    Listening…                  Escuchando…             ____________________
    Heard                       Se oye                  ____________________
    Louder                      Más fuerte              ____________________
    No microphone               Sin micrófono           ____________________

    THE TWO WE'RE LEAST SURE OF:

    "Heard" — this is the good state, the reward. It should feel like "you're
    coming through," not like a passive technical report. We used "Se oye."
    Other candidates we rejected: "Te oyen" (uses tú), "Se le oye" (correct
    but clumsy), "Claro." If one of those is better, say so.

    "Louder" — this is a nudge, not a scolding. "Más fuerte" is literal and
    short. Is there something warmer that still fits in the same space?

    "Escuchando la sala" — "sala" may be wrong here. It means the app is
    measuring the background noise of wherever you are, which might be a
    kitchen, a restaurant, or a car. Is there a better word than "sala"?


PART 2 — HOW LOUD YOU NEED TO BE
=============================================================================
Five steps from easiest to hardest. The NAMES appear as buttons [TIGHT]; the
notes appear underneath in small text.

    Section heading:
    How loud you need to be   →   Qué tan fuerte hablar      ______________

    Explanation:
    "Pick something you can already manage. Raise it later."
    →  "Elija algo que ya pueda lograr. Súbalo más adelante."
                                                              ______________

    The notes now carry a dB number, because the app targets normal
    conversational volume (60-70 dB) and not merely "louder than the room".

    Name [TIGHT]      Note                                Your correction
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Gentle            below conversational — a starting point
    Suave             por debajo de lo conversacional — para empezar
                                                          ____________________
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Easy              a relaxed voice, about 60 dB
    Fácil             una voz relajada, unos 60 dB        ____________________
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Normal            conversational, about 63 dB
    Normal            conversacional, unos 63 dB          ____________________
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Firm              projecting, about 66 dB
    Firme             proyectando, unos 66 dB             ____________________
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Strong            strong projection, about 69 dB
    Fuerte            proyección fuerte, unos 69 dB       ____________________

    Explanation under the five:
    "Normal conversation is 60-70 dB. The dot needs your voice above the room
     AND above this level, so a quiet room no longer lets a whisper through."
    →  "Una conversación normal está entre 60 y 70 dB. El punto necesita que
        su voz esté por encima de la sala Y por encima de este nivel, para que
        una sala silenciosa ya no permita un susurro."
                                                          ____________________

    CHECK: "dB" is left as "dB" — the international symbol. Confirm that's
    what a Spanish speaker would expect to see, rather than "decibeles".

    IMPORTANT: these five names must feel ENCOURAGING, not like commands. In
    English "Strong" is a description of a setting, not an order to be strong.
    If "Fuerte" reads as an instruction — or worse, as a judgement about the
    person — please suggest something else. This matters more than literal
    accuracy.

    Also: "encender" (to light up) is used for the dot lighting up. If that
    metaphor doesn't work in Spanish, tell us — we can change the English too.


PART 3 — IN THE CAR
=============================================================================
Safety text. Accuracy matters more than elegance here.

    Heading:
    In the car   →   En el auto                                ______________

    Explanation:
    "A phone cannot tell a driver from a passenger, so it has to ask. This
    resets every time the app starts."
    →  "El teléfono no puede distinguir si usted conduce o va de pasajero,
        así que tiene que preguntar. Esto se reinicia cada vez que abre la
        aplicación."
                                                              ______________

    The three choices — these are the USER speaking about themselves, so they
    are in the first person:

    Not in a car        →  No voy en auto                      ______________
    I'm a passenger     →  Voy de pasajero                     ______________
    I'm driving         →  Voy conduciendo                     ______________

    Their explanations:

    "The dot stays on — nobody is steering."
    →  "El punto sigue encendido: nadie va al volante."        ______________

    "Screen stays dark. A short buzz when your voice drops."
    →  "La pantalla queda oscura. Una vibración corta cuando su voz baja."
                                                              ______________

    On the dark screen while driving:
    Driving — buzz only  →  Conduciendo — solo vibración       ______________
    tap to change        →  toque para cambiar                 ______________

    CHECK CAREFULLY: "Voy conduciendo" vs "Estoy conduciendo" vs "Voy
    manejando." "Manejar" is more common than "conducir" in much of Latin
    America — should we switch? This is the one place where being
    misunderstood has real consequences.

    NOTE ON "pill": in English this describes the SHAPE — a small rounded bar.
    We used "barra pequeña" (small bar) because "píldora" would suggest
    medication, which is badly wrong for an app aimed at people taking daily
    medication. If there's a better word for a small floating bar, tell us.

PART 4c — MICROPHONE CALIBRATION
=============================================================================
An advanced setting, aimed at a therapist with a real sound level meter rather
than at the patient. It should read as "you can safely ignore this".

    Heading:
    MICROPHONE CALIBRATION  →  CALIBRACIÓN DEL MICRÓFONO   ______________

    "Only worth touching if the dot seems far too easy or far too hard
     everywhere. Speak normally at arm's length and compare with a sound level
     meter app."
    →  "Solo vale la pena cambiarla si el punto parece demasiado fácil o
        demasiado difícil en todas partes. Hable normalmente a un brazo de
        distancia y compare con una aplicación de sonómetro."
                                                          ______________

    Now: about 63 dB   →  Ahora: unos 63 dB                ______________
    Now: quiet         →  Ahora: silencio                  ______________
    Bar: 63 dB         →  Nivel: 63 dB                     ______________
    Reset              →  Restablecer                      ______________

    CHECK: "sonómetro" for a sound level meter — is that the word people
    actually use, or would "medidor de decibeles" be clearer?

    CHECK: "a un brazo de distancia" for "at arm's length". Natural?
