PDVoice Buddy — Windows and Mac
A light that stays on while your voice is carrying


WHAT IT DOES
------------

Parkinson's makes voices quieter without the person noticing. You feel like
you're speaking normally; the room hears a whisper.

PDVoice Buddy listens to the room and shows you one thing: a glowing dot.
Bright means your voice is carrying. Dim means it's fading, and it's time to
push a little.

That's the whole app. No exercises, no scores, nothing to read while you're
mid-sentence.

It works out how loud the room is and asks you to rise above it. That means it
adjusts itself — a quiet study and a room with the television on both work,
without you changing anything.

This is the same app as the tablet version, with the same measurement and the
same settings.


STARTING IT
-----------

Double-click PDVoice Buddy.

The first time, Windows or your Mac may ask to allow the microphone. Say yes.

Nothing is ever recorded. It measures how loud the room is, about twenty times
a second, keeps that one number, and throws the sound away. There is no
recording to find, because none is ever made.

If nothing appears to happen, run "Show errors" instead — it keeps the window
open so any message can be read.


THE FIRST FEW SECONDS
---------------------

When it starts it says "Listening to the room" for a second or two. That's it
learning what your room sounds like with nobody talking.

If you move the computer to a different room, or the room gets much noisier,
pause and unpause it so it relearns.


USING IT
--------

Just talk. Watch the dot out of the corner of your eye.

  Bright and glowing ......... They can hear you. Keep going.
  Dim and small .............. You're fading. Speak up.
  Growing and shrinking ...... It follows every word, so you can see
                               yourself getting louder before you reach
                               the target.

Don't stare at it. The dot is meant for the corner of your eye, while your
attention stays on the person you're talking to.


THE BUTTONS
-----------

  Pause (two bars) ..... Stops it listening. Press it for private calls, or
                         when you're watching something. Press again — it
                         becomes a triangle — to start listening again.

  Gear ................. Settings.

  ✕ .................... Hides it. It keeps listening, and the icon stays
                         near the clock. Click that icon to bring it back.
                         To stop it completely, use Quit in settings, or
                         right-click the icon near the clock.

On the full-screen size you can also click anywhere to open settings, or press
Esc to hide.


SETTINGS
--------

Four things, and you'll probably only ever change the first.

HOW LOUD YOU NEED TO BE
    Five choices, from Gentle to Strong.

    Pick one you can already manage. If the dot is dark most of the time,
    the setting is too high — go down a step. You want a dot that's lit more
    often than not.

    If you're working with a speech therapist, ask them where to set it, and
    raise it a step every week or two rather than all at once.

COLOUR
    Mint, amber, sky or white. Whichever you notice most easily.

SIZE
    Small pill — a narrow bar that floats on top of everything else, so you
    can use the computer normally. Drag it anywhere; it remembers where you
    left it.

    Full screen — a large dot filling the screen. Good for a second monitor,
    or a laptop parked on the table during a conversation.

OPEN AT LOGIN
    Starts it automatically when you turn the computer on, so there's nothing
    to remember. Recommended.


LEAVING IT RUNNING
------------------

It's meant to be left on. Hiding it with ✕ keeps it listening.

The icon near the clock — on Windows you may need to click the small arrow to
see hidden icons — shows it's running. Click it to show or hide the app,
right-click it for Settings and Quit.


IF SOMETHING'S WRONG
--------------------

The dot never lights up, even when I speak up
    The setting is probably too high. Open settings and go down a step or
    two — try Easy or Gentle.

The dot is always lit, even when I whisper
    Something in the room is noisy — a television, a fan, a fan inside the
    computer itself. Turn it off if you can, then pause and unpause so it
    relearns the room.

It says "Listening to the room" and stays that way
    Give it two seconds. If it doesn't change, pause and unpause.

It says "No microphone"
    Windows: Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone, and make sure
    desktop apps are allowed.
    Mac: System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone.

The dot doesn't react at all
    If you have several microphones — a webcam, a headset, a docking
    station — the computer may be listening through one that's facing away
    from you. Change the default input device in your sound settings.

I lost it
    Look for the icon near the clock, behind the small arrow for hidden
    icons. Click it.


ONE HONEST NOTE
---------------

This is a homemade tool, not a medical device.

It's good at telling louder from quieter in one place. It is not a
measurement, and it should not be used to make any decision about treatment.

Use it as a reminder. Set the level with your speech-language pathologist
rather than guessing, especially if you're already doing speech therapy.

If the dot helps you stay audible, that's the whole point. If it turns into
one more thing nagging at you, close it and say so — it can be changed.
