PDVOICE BUDDY — INSTALLING ON ANDROID
User-facing paragraph. Copy this wherever you're handing out the app.

IMPORTANT FOR WHOEVER PUBLISHES THIS: Android blocks a hand-installed app in
THREE separate places, and each one uses alarming language. All three have to
be named in advance or people will stop, assume the app is dangerous, and
delete it. Do not trim these warnings out to make the copy shorter.


MAIN VERSION — for a download page or an email
==============================================

PDVoice Buddy isn't in the Play Store yet, so installing it takes a few extra
taps — and Android will try to stop you three times along the way. None of the
warnings mean anything is wrong; they appear for every app that doesn't come
from the store. First, when you download the file, your browser may say the
file "can't be scanned" or "may be harmful" — choose Download anyway. Second,
when you open the downloaded file, Android may say your browser isn't allowed
to install apps; tap Settings on that message, switch on "Allow from this
source", and press Back. Third, just before it installs, you may see a screen
about an "unsafe app" or "unknown developer" — tap "Install anyway" or "More
details" and then Install. After that it installs normally. Open PDVoice
Buddy, allow the microphone when asked, and you're running.


WITH THE FIRST-RUN STEPS INCLUDED
=================================

PDVoice Buddy isn't in the Play Store yet, so installing it takes a few extra
taps — and Android will try to stop you three times along the way. None of the
warnings mean anything is wrong; they appear for every app that doesn't come
from the store. First, when you download the file, your browser may say the
file "can't be scanned" or "may be harmful" — choose Download anyway. Second,
when you open the downloaded file, Android may say your browser isn't allowed
to install apps; tap Settings on that message, switch on "Allow from this
source", and press Back. Third, just before it installs, you may see a screen
about an "unsafe app" or "unknown developer" — tap "Install anyway" or "More
details" and then Install. Once it's installed, open PDVoice Buddy and allow
the microphone when asked — without that, the light can't work. A glowing dot
fills the screen, and after a second or two it's listening. Just talk and
watch the dot: bright means your voice is carrying, dim means speak up.


STEP-BY-STEP VERSION — if your testers would rather have a list
===============================================================

1. Tap the download link. If your browser warns that the file can't be
   scanned or may be harmful, choose "Download anyway".

2. Open the downloaded file — tap it in the notification, or find it in your
   Files app under Downloads.

3. If Android says your browser isn't allowed to install apps, tap
   "Settings" on that message, switch on "Allow from this source", then
   press Back.

4. If you see a warning about an unsafe app or an unknown developer, tap
   "More details" or "Install anyway", then Install.
   (On some tablets this is worded as "Play Protect doesn't recognise this
   developer". Same thing.)

5. Tap Open, and allow the microphone when asked.

You'll see a glowing dot. That's it — just talk.


VERY SHORT — one or two lines
=============================

PDVoice Buddy isn't in the Play Store yet, so Android will warn you two or
three times while installing — about scanning the file, about allowing your
browser to install apps, and about an unknown developer. All of that is normal
for an app installed by hand; choose the "anyway" option each time. Allow the
microphone when it asks, and you're running.


IF SOMEONE GETS STUCK
=====================

"There's no Download anyway option"
    Pull down the notification shade and tap the download notification, or
    open Files > Downloads and tap the file there.

"It says the app wasn't installed"
    Usually an older copy is already on the device. Uninstall PDVoice Buddy
    first, then install again.

"It says the file is corrupt or can't be opened"
    The download didn't finish. Delete it from Downloads and try again on
    Wi-Fi.

"I can't find Allow from this source"
    Settings > Apps > Special app access > Install unknown apps > pick the
    browser you downloaded with > switch it on.

"Play Protect won't let me"
    Tap "More details" first — the Install anyway option is hidden until you
    do. If it's genuinely absent, Play Store > tap your profile picture >
    Play Protect > gear icon > turn off scanning, install, then turn it back
    on.


A NOTE ON TONE
==============

Say plainly that the warnings are expected, and say it BEFORE describing the
steps. Someone managing a health condition, being told by their own tablet
that an app may be harmful, will very reasonably stop — and they should, unless
someone they trust has told them in advance exactly which warnings to expect.

Don't dismiss the warnings as pointless. They exist for good reasons. The
honest framing is: Android can't vouch for an app that didn't come from the
store, so it warns you — you're relying on trusting whoever gave it to you.
