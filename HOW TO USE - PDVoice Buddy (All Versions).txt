PDVOICE BUDDY
A light that stays on while your voice is carrying

For tablets and phones (Android) and for computers (Windows and Mac).


=============================================================================
WHAT IT DOES
=============================================================================

Parkinson's makes voices quieter without the person noticing. You feel like
you're speaking normally; the room hears a whisper.

PDVoice Buddy listens to the room and shows you one thing: a glowing dot.
Bright means your voice is carrying. Dim means it's fading, and it's time to
push a little.

That's the whole app. No exercises, no scores, nothing to read while you're
mid-sentence.

It works out how loud the room is and asks you to rise above it — and it also
asks you to reach a normal conversational level, 60–70 dB. You have to clear
both. That means a busy café asks more of you than a quiet kitchen, but a
silent room still won't accept a whisper.

Nothing is ever recorded. It measures how loud the room is, about twenty times
a second, keeps that one number, and throws the sound away. There is no
recording to find, because none is ever made.

The tablet version and the computer version are the same app, with the same
measurement and the same settings.

It works in English and Spanish. Normally it just matches your device, so
there is nothing to do — but if your device is set to English and you'd
rather read Spanish, you can change it in settings. See Part 4.


=============================================================================
PART 1 — GETTING IT ONTO A TABLET OR PHONE (ANDROID)
=============================================================================

PDVoice Buddy isn't in the Play Store yet, so Android will try to stop you
three times while installing. None of the warnings mean anything is wrong —
they appear for every app that doesn't come from the store.

  1. Tap the download link. If your browser says the file can't be scanned
     or may be harmful, choose "Download anyway".

  2. Open the downloaded file — tap it in the notification, or find it in
     Files > Downloads.

  3. If Android says your browser isn't allowed to install apps, tap
     "Settings" on that message, switch on "Allow from this source", then
     press Back.

  4. If you see a warning about an unsafe app or an unknown developer, tap
     "More details" or "Install anyway", then Install.

  5. Tap Open, and allow the microphone when asked.

If you get stuck:

  No "Download anyway" option
      Pull down the notification shade and tap the download, or open
      Files > Downloads and tap the file there.

  "App wasn't installed"
      An older copy is already there. Uninstall PDVoice Buddy, then retry.

  Can't find "Allow from this source"
      Settings > Apps > Special app access > Install unknown apps > choose
      your browser > switch it on.

  Play Protect won't let me
      Tap "More details" first — the Install anyway option is hidden until
      you do.


=============================================================================
PART 2 — GETTING IT ONTO A COMPUTER (WINDOWS)
=============================================================================

There's no real installation — it's a single file.

  1. Download PDVoice Buddy.exe and put it somewhere you'll find again.
     Your Desktop is fine.

  2. Double-click it.

  3. Windows will probably show a blue box saying "Windows protected your
     PC". This is expected — it only means the app doesn't come from a big
     software company. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".

  4. Allow the microphone when asked, or the light can't work.

A small pill appears in the corner of your screen. After a second or two it's
listening.

To make it easier next time: open the gear and turn on "Open at login". Then
it starts by itself and there's nothing to remember.


=============================================================================
PART 3 — USING IT (BOTH VERSIONS)
=============================================================================

THE FIRST FEW SECONDS

When it starts it says "Listening to the room" for a second or two. That's it
learning what your room sounds like with nobody talking.

If you move to a different room, or the room gets much noisier, pause and
unpause it so it relearns.


JUST TALK

Watch the dot out of the corner of your eye.

  Bright and glowing ......... They can hear you. Keep going.
  Dim and small .............. You're fading. Speak up.
  Growing and shrinking ...... It follows every word, so you can see
                               yourself getting louder before you reach
                               the target.

Don't stare at it. The dot is meant for the corner of your eye, while your
attention stays on the person you're talking to.


THE BUTTONS

  Pause (two bars) ..... Stops it listening. Press it for private calls, or
                         when you're watching television. Press again — it
                         becomes a triangle — to start listening again.

  Gear ................. Settings.

  ✕ .................... Tablet: closes the app.
                         Computer: hides it, still listening. The icon stays
                         near the clock — click that to bring it back. To
                         stop it completely, use Quit in settings.

On the full-screen size you can also tap or click anywhere to open settings.
On a computer, Esc hides it.


=============================================================================
PART 4 — SETTINGS
=============================================================================

Seven settings, and you'll probably only ever change the first. Microphone
calibration is the one to leave alone unless a therapist asks you to.

("Where to put the device" below isn't a setting — it's placement advice, and
it's worth reading.)


HOW LOUD YOU NEED TO BE

Five choices, from Gentle to Strong.

Normal conversation sits between 60 and 70 dB, and that's what these are built
around:

    Gentle    about 57 dB    just below conversational, for starting out
    Easy      about 60 dB    a relaxed voice
    Normal    about 63 dB    conversational — where most people should be
    Firm      about 66 dB    projecting deliberately
    Strong    about 69 dB    strong projection

The dot needs your voice to clear TWO bars at once: above the room, and above
the level in that list. In a noisy restaurant the room is what's holding you
back. In a silent living room the level is — which is the point, because being
louder than a silent room proves nothing.

Pick one you can already manage. If the dot is dark most of the time, the
setting is too high — go down a step. You want a dot that's lit more often
than not.

If you're working with a speech therapist, ask them where to set it, and
raise it a step every week or two rather than all at once.


WHERE TO PUT THE DEVICE

About an arm's length away — roughly three feet, or a metre.

This matters more than it used to. A microphone hears a quieter sound the
further away it is, so a tablet across the room will think you're too quiet
when you aren't, and one right under your chin will think you're fine when
you're not.

On the table in front of you is right. On a shelf across the room is not.


COLOUR

Mint, amber, sky or white. Whichever you notice most easily.


SIZE

Full screen — a large dot in the middle. Easiest to see across a room. On a
tablet this can also run as the screensaver, so a docked tablet becomes a
permanent desk light with nothing to launch.

Small pill — a narrow bar that floats on top of whatever else you're doing,
so you can use the device normally. Drag it wherever you like; it remembers.
It has its own pause, gear and ✕ buttons.

On a tablet, the first time you choose Small pill, Android will send you to a
settings screen to allow it. Allow it, then come back — the pill will be
running.


MICROPHONE CALIBRATION

Leave this alone unless the dot is wrong everywhere you go.

Every device's microphone is a little different, and none of them are real
sound level meters. This setting is the app's guess at how to turn what the
microphone hears into a dB number. It shows the current reading and lets you
nudge it up or down a dB at a time.

It's here for a therapist with a proper sound level meter — or a free meter
app — who wants the numbers to line up. If the dot behaves sensibly for you,
there is nothing to do here.

If you change it and regret it, press Reset.


LANGUAGE

English or Spanish.

"Automatic" means it follows whatever your phone, tablet or computer is set
to. That's how it arrives, and for most people it's already correct.

Change it if the device is set to one language but you'd rather read the
other — which is common when somebody else set the device up for you. The
choices are shown in their own language, so "Español" reads as Español no
matter which language the app is currently in.

The change happens immediately. Nothing restarts, and nothing else is
affected.


OPEN AT LOGIN (computer only)

Starts it automatically when you turn the computer on. Recommended.


IN THE CAR (tablet and phone only)

A device cannot tell whether you're driving or in the passenger seat, so it
has to ask.

  Not in a car ...... normal.
  I'm a passenger ... the dot stays on. Nobody is steering.
  I'm driving ....... the screen goes dark and you get a short buzz when
                      your voice drops. Nothing to look at.

IMPORTANT: this resets every time you open the app. If you're going to drive,
set it to "I'm driving" each time. A moving light in a driver's line of sight
is dangerous and against the law in many places.


=============================================================================
PART 5 — LEAVING IT RUNNING
=============================================================================

TABLET OR PHONE

While it's listening there's a notice in the top bar. That's Android's rule,
not a choice — any app using the microphone has to show one. Tap it to come
back to PDVoice Buddy.

To stop it completely: the ✕ on the pill, or close the app.


COMPUTER

It's meant to be left on. Hiding it with ✕ keeps it listening.

The icon near the clock — on Windows you may need to click the small arrow to
see hidden icons — shows it's running. Click it to show or hide, right-click
for Settings and Quit.


=============================================================================
PART 6 — IF SOMETHING'S WRONG
=============================================================================

The dot never lights up, even when I speak up
    First check where the device is — an arm's length away, not across the
    room. Then, if it's still dark, the setting is probably too high. Open
    settings and go down a step or two.

The dot lights up too easily
    Check the device isn't right under your chin. If it's placed sensibly and
    still too easy, go up a step.

The dot is always lit, even when I whisper
    Something in the room is noisy — a television, a fan, a dishwasher, or
    the fan inside the computer itself. Turn it off if you can, then pause
    and unpause so it relearns the room.

It says "Listening to the room" and stays that way
    Give it two seconds. If it doesn't change, pause and unpause.

It says "No microphone", or the dot doesn't react at all
    Check the microphone is allowed:
      Android:  Settings > Apps > PDVoice Buddy > Permissions > Microphone
      Windows:  Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone > allow
                desktop apps
      Mac:      System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone

    On a computer, if you have several microphones — a webcam, a headset, a
    docking station — it may be listening through one facing away from you.
    Change the default input device in your sound settings.

I chose Small pill on the tablet and nothing happened
    Android needs permission for a floating pill. Tap Small pill again and
    it'll take you to the right screen.

I can't find the pill
    Tablet: open the app from its icon, switch to Full screen, then back to
    Small pill.
    Computer: look for the icon near the clock, behind the arrow for hidden
    icons. Click it.


=============================================================================
ONE HONEST NOTE
=============================================================================

This is a homemade tool, not a medical device.

It's good at telling louder from quieter in one place, and at nudging you
toward a conversational level. The dB numbers are approximations from an
uncalibrated microphone, not measurements — they will not match a real sound
level meter, and no decision about treatment should rest on them.

Use it as a reminder. Set the level with your speech-language pathologist
rather than guessing, especially if you're already doing speech therapy.

If the dot helps you stay audible, that's the whole point. If it turns into
one more thing nagging at you, close it and say so — it can be changed.
